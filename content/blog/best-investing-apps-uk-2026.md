The UK investing app market has matured significantly. Commission-free trading is now the baseline expectation, not a differentiator. What actually separates the best platforms in 2026 is the quality of their ISA wrapper, the breadth of their investment universe, the reliability of their execution, and — most importantly — whether the platform is regulated to a standard that protects your money when things go wrong.

This comparison covers the five most-used investing apps in the UK, from pure-play trading apps to established fund platforms.

## Quick verdict

**Choose Freetrade** if you want a regulated UK stocks and shares ISA with commission-free trading and a clean, beginner-friendly interface. Best for long-term ISA investors buying UK and US stocks.

**Choose Trading 212** if you want the widest selection of stocks, fractional shares from £1, and the highest interest rate on uninvested cash. Best for active investors and those building diversified global portfolios.

**Choose eToro** if you want to copy top-performing investors automatically or want social trading features alongside standard investing. Best for beginners who want guided exposure to markets.

**Choose Hargreaves Lansdown** if you want the most comprehensive platform, widest fund selection, and the strongest track record for reliability. Best for serious investors who value breadth and security over the lowest fees.

**Choose Vanguard** if you only want low-cost index funds and ETFs and are happy with a limited but extremely well-priced selection. Best for passive long-term investors.

## The ISA question

For UK investors, the Stocks and Shares ISA wrapper is the most important consideration. All investment gains and income inside an ISA are free from UK tax. The annual ISA allowance is £20,000 per person.

All five platforms in this comparison offer a Stocks and Shares ISA. The differences:

**Freetrade** charges £5.99/month for ISA access on its Standard plan, or it is included in Plus at £9.99/month. The fee is flat regardless of portfolio size, which makes it cost-effective for larger portfolios and expensive for small ones.

**Trading 212** offers a completely free ISA — no monthly fee, no percentage charge. This is genuinely unusual and makes it the lowest-cost ISA option for most retail investors.

**eToro** offers a free ISA available to UK users, also with no annual fee.

**Hargreaves Lansdown** charges a platform fee of 0.45% per year (capped at £45/year for shares) for its ISA. For a £10,000 portfolio, this is £45/year. For a £50,000 portfolio, it is still £45/year on shares (the cap kicks in). For funds, the fee scales up to 0.45% with no cap until £250,000.

**Vanguard** charges 0.15% per year platform fee (capped at £375/year), making it extremely cost-effective for larger portfolios invested in Vanguard funds.

The spread on eToro is important to understand: eToro does not charge a commission but makes money on the bid-ask spread. For liquid stocks like Apple or Tesla, this is typically 0.09-0.15%. For less liquid assets, it can be higher. This is not free trading — it is differently-structured trading costs.

## Investment selection

**Trading 212** has the widest selection with 12,000+ stocks and ETFs across 17 exchanges. If you want to buy a specific stock, Trading 212 almost certainly has it.

**Freetrade** lists approximately 6,000 stocks across UK, US, and European exchanges. Missing some smaller companies but covers most of what retail investors actually want.

**eToro** has a broad selection but the interface prioritises its CopyTrader and social features. The actual stock universe is smaller than Trading 212 or Freetrade for pure stock picking.

**Hargreaves Lansdown** has the most comprehensive fund universe with 3,000+ funds alongside stocks and ETFs. If you want actively managed funds, investment trusts, or specific bond products, HL is the only platform in this list that covers them properly.

**Vanguard** only offers its own funds and ETFs — approximately 80 products. If you want anything outside Vanguard's range, you cannot get it here.

## Interest on uninvested cash

This has become a meaningful differentiator as interest rates have risen.

**Trading 212** pays up to 5.2% AER on uninvested cash in ISA and invest accounts — the highest of any major UK investing platform. This is genuinely valuable if you keep significant cash in your account between investments.

**Freetrade** pays interest on uninvested cash on its paid plans.

**eToro** pays interest on uninvested USD balances but the rate is lower.

**Hargreaves Lansdown** pays interest on cash held in accounts but at a rate lower than Trading 212.

**Vanguard** holds uninvested cash in a money market fund.

## Regulation and investor protection

All five platforms are regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) and covered by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS) up to £85,000 per person.

**Hargreaves Lansdown** is the most established with 40+ years of operation and approximately £140 billion in assets under administration. Its regulatory track record is the strongest in this comparison.

**Freetrade** and **Trading 212** are newer but FCA regulated with clean track records. Both have grown significantly and have no major regulatory incidents.

**eToro** has had some regulatory actions in other markets. Its UK entity is FCA regulated and UK customer assets are protected under FSCS.

## Who should use what

**New investor with under £5,000:** Trading 212. Free ISA, fractional shares from £1, good interest on cash, widest selection.

**Long-term ISA investor building a portfolio:** Freetrade for stock picking or Vanguard if you want pure passive index investing at the lowest possible cost.

**Someone who wants to copy successful investors:** eToro. Its CopyTrader feature is unique and well-executed.

**Serious investor with £50,000+:** Hargreaves Lansdown. The platform fee caps at a reasonable level, the fund selection is unmatched, and the reliability and regulatory standing justify the higher per-trade cost.

**Pure passive investor who just wants index funds:** Vanguard. Its fees are the lowest available for its own funds and the platform is built for exactly this use case.

## The bottom line

For most UK retail investors starting out in 2026, Trading 212 is the best starting point: free ISA, widest stock selection, highest cash interest, fractional shares. As your portfolio grows and your needs become more sophisticated — particularly if you want actively managed funds, investment trusts, or the highest level of platform reliability — Hargreaves Lansdown earns its higher fees.

The worst outcome is not investing at all because the choice feels complicated. Any of these five platforms is dramatically better than leaving money in a current account.
