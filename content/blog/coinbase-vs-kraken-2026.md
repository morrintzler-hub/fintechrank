If you are based in the US or UK and want to buy, sell, or hold cryptocurrency with regulatory protection, your realistic options narrow quickly. Binance is restricted. Bybit and OKX do not serve US or UK customers. That leaves Coinbase and Kraken as the two primary choices for regulated crypto trading in the English-speaking world.

They are more different than they appear. This comparison tells you which one is actually right for you.

## Quick verdict

**Choose Coinbase** if you are new to crypto, want the simplest experience, or prioritise the strongest regulatory standing available in a crypto company. Accept that you will pay more in fees.

**Choose Kraken** if you have some crypto experience, are cost-conscious, want access to more coins and staking, or are a more active trader who wants lower fees without sacrificing regulatory protection.

**The case for using both:** Coinbase for easy purchases and long-term holdings; Kraken for trading, staking, and lower fees on frequent transactions.

## Regulatory standing

Both companies are extensively regulated, but there are meaningful differences.

**Coinbase** is publicly listed on Nasdaq — the only major crypto exchange in the world to have completed an IPO on a US stock exchange. This subjects it to SEC disclosure requirements, quarterly earnings reports, and the full scrutiny that comes with being a public company. Coinbase holds Money Transmitter Licences across all 50 US states, a Virtual Asset Service Provider registration in the UK, and licences in multiple other jurisdictions.

**Kraken** was founded in 2011 and is one of the oldest operating exchanges. It has never suffered a major exchange-level hack — a 15-year track record in an industry where exchange breaches have been distressingly common. Kraken holds money transmission licences across the US and multiple European countries. In 2023, it established Kraken Bank, a Wyoming-chartered Special Purpose Depository Institution, making it one of the few crypto companies with an actual bank charter.

Both are among the most heavily regulated crypto companies in the world. For practical purposes, the regulatory protection each offers is broadly equivalent for everyday users.

## Fees: the biggest difference

Fees are where Coinbase and Kraken diverge most meaningfully, and where the most money is unnecessarily lost by users who do not understand the fee structure.

**Coinbase** has two interfaces with very different fee structures. The standard Coinbase app charges 0.5% to 4.5% per transaction depending on payment method and transaction size — these are among the highest fees of any reputable exchange. Coinbase Advanced Trade (the professional interface, available free to all accounts) charges 0.6% taker and 0.4% maker at standard volumes, dropping to 0.4%/0.2% at higher volumes.

The most important thing to know about Coinbase fees: if you are using the standard app interface, you are paying 5-10x more than you need to. Switch to Advanced Trade immediately.

**Kraken** charges 0.26% taker and 0.16% maker on its Pro interface at standard volumes — lower than Coinbase Advanced Trade at equivalent volume. On Kraken's basic interface (Instant Buy), fees are higher (1.5% spread), but the Pro interface is easily accessible and most users should use it.

On a £1,000 Bitcoin purchase:
- Coinbase standard app: approximately £35-45 in fees
- Coinbase Advanced Trade: approximately £6
- Kraken Pro: approximately £2.60

The difference between using Coinbase's standard app and Kraken Pro for a year of regular purchases can easily exceed £200-300 on a modest investment amount.

## Coin selection

**Kraken** lists approximately 200-250 coins, including most assets with meaningful market capitalisation. Its selection is curated rather than comprehensive — Kraken applies relatively stringent listing standards that filter out the most speculative assets.

**Coinbase** lists approximately 240 coins. Like Kraken, it applies listing standards that exclude many low-cap speculative tokens. The overlap between the two is significant — the major coins you are most likely to want are available on both platforms.

Neither platform is the right choice if you want to trade obscure altcoins or newly launched tokens. For those, you would need to look at Binance or a decentralised exchange.

## Staking

**Kraken** offers one of the strongest staking products of any regulated exchange. You can stake Ethereum, Polkadot, Cosmos, Tezos, and approximately 15 other assets directly on the platform with competitive yields. Staking rewards are paid weekly. Kraken's staking product has a strong track record for reliability and yield delivery.

Note: Kraken's US staking product was shut down in 2023 following an SEC settlement and a £30 million fine. US users lost access to on-exchange staking. UK and European users are unaffected.

**Coinbase** offers staking for a smaller selection of assets. It launched its own liquid staking token (cbETH) and has invested in its staking infrastructure. Yields are slightly lower than Kraken's historically, though the gap varies by asset.

## Security features

Both platforms offer strong security tooling.

**Coinbase** provides two-factor authentication, biometric login, device management, and a dedicated Vault product for long-term holdings that requires multiple approvals and a time delay to withdraw. Approximately 98% of customer assets are stored in cold storage.

**Kraken** offers two-factor authentication, global settings lock (which prevents changes to account settings for a set time period), master key authentication, and withdrawal address whitelisting. Its security practices are industry-leading and its 15-year clean track record reflects sustained operational discipline.

## User experience

**Coinbase** has the better consumer-facing interface. The standard app is clean, well-designed, and appropriate for beginners. Charts are readable without being overwhelming. The onboarding process is smooth. Coinbase's educational content (Coinbase Earn) allows users to earn small amounts of crypto by completing lessons — a genuinely useful feature for newcomers.

**Kraken** has improved its interface significantly but remains more complex for beginners. The Pro trading interface provides depth of market, advanced chart types, and order management tools that experienced traders value. For a first-time buyer, Kraken's interface requires more confidence to navigate than Coinbase's.

## Customer support

**Kraken** has historically had better-rated customer support than Coinbase, which has faced criticism for slow response times and automated responses on complex issues. Both have improved, but Kraken's support team is generally considered more knowledgeable and responsive for technical issues.

## The verdict by user type

**Total beginner:** Coinbase, but switch to Advanced Trade before your first purchase. The clean interface and educational content make it the right starting point. Just do not use the standard fee structure.

**Cost-conscious buyer who trades regularly:** Kraken Pro. The fee difference compounds significantly over time.

**UK user who wants staking:** Kraken. Its staking product is more comprehensive and the yields are competitive.

**Institutional or high-net-worth individual:** Both offer institutional products — Coinbase Prime and Kraken Institutional. Coinbase's is more mature.

**Someone who wants the maximum regulatory protection possible:** Coinbase, on the basis of being the only publicly listed major exchange. Its disclosure requirements provide a level of transparency no other crypto company offers.

The most common mistake new crypto buyers make is using Coinbase's standard app interface without realising how much extra they are paying. The second most common mistake is keeping large holdings on any exchange long-term. Use an exchange to buy; use a hardware wallet to hold.
