Wise and Payoneer are the two most commonly recommended platforms for freelancers, contractors, and remote workers who need to receive payments from clients in other countries. They solve similar problems but work very differently — and choosing the wrong one costs real money over time.

This is the honest comparison, based on what each platform actually charges rather than what their marketing pages say.

## Quick verdict

**Choose Wise** if your clients pay you via bank transfer and you want the best exchange rates available. Wise's mid-market rate with a transparent fee consistently beats Payoneer for pure currency conversion.

**Choose Payoneer** if you receive payments from online marketplaces (Upwork, Fiverr, Amazon, Airbnb) or platforms that have a built-in Payoneer integration, since withdrawals from these platforms to Payoneer are often free or very low cost.

**Use both** if you have marketplace income and also receive direct client bank transfers — many freelancers maintain both accounts.

## What each platform is actually for

**Wise** was built as a currency transfer service for individuals who were tired of banks hiding fees in bad exchange rates. Its core promise has never changed: give you the mid-market rate (the rate you see on Google) with a transparent percentage fee on top. Wise expanded into multi-currency accounts, debit cards, and business banking, but currency conversion remains what it does best.

**Payoneer** was built primarily as a payment receiving platform for online marketplace sellers and freelancers. Its original use case was: Amazon sellers and Upwork freelancers need a way to get paid from US-based platforms without a US bank account. Payoneer built relationships with these platforms so they could pay out directly to Payoneer accounts, often for free or at very low cost. It then expanded into global payments more broadly.

Understanding these different origins explains the key differences in how they work today.

## Fees: the real comparison

### Receiving payments

**Wise** charges to receive payments in some currencies. Receiving GBP via local bank transfer is free. Receiving USD via ACH is free. Receiving EUR via SEPA is free. Receiving via SWIFT international wire costs £4.14 per transfer plus a percentage.

**Payoneer** charges 1% to receive payments from clients not on the Payoneer network. Receiving from marketplace partners (Upwork, Fiverr, Amazon, etc.) is typically free or very low cost under a special arrangement.

**The practical implication:** If your clients pay you via Upwork or another Payoneer-integrated platform, Payoneer receiving costs are lower. If your clients pay via direct bank transfer, Wise receiving costs are lower.

### Currency conversion

This is where the difference matters most.

**Wise** uses the mid-market rate for all currency conversions. On a £1,000 conversion from USD to GBP, Wise charges approximately 0.41-0.6% — typically £4-6 in fees. What you see is what you pay. No hidden spread.

**Payoneer** uses a rate that includes a margin above the mid-market rate. Payoneer's USD to GBP conversion typically applies a 2% spread above the mid-market rate. On a $1,000 conversion, this is approximately $20 in hidden fees versus Wise's $5-6 in explicit fees.

On an annual income of $50,000 converted from USD to GBP, the difference between Wise and Payoneer's conversion costs is approximately $700-800 per year. This is significant.

### Withdrawal to bank account

**Wise:** Withdrawing from your Wise account to your local bank account is free for most currencies. The money is already in local currency if you converted it — it is simply a bank transfer.

**Payoneer:** Withdrawing from your Payoneer account to a local bank account costs 2% for most currencies. On a $5,000 withdrawal, this is $100.

## Multi-currency accounts

**Wise** gives you local bank account details in 10 currencies: GBP (sort code and account number), EUR (IBAN), USD (routing and account number), AUD, NZD, CAD, SGD, HUF, RON, and TRY. This means you can give a US client a real US bank account number to pay you, just like a local supplier.

**Payoneer** gives you receiving accounts in USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, AUD, CAD, and MXN. Similar concept, slightly fewer currencies than Wise.

For most freelancers, the practical coverage is equivalent — both have USD, EUR, and GBP covered.

## Debit cards

**Wise** issues a debit card (Wise card) that converts at the mid-market rate with a small fee on currency conversion. Excellent for travel and international purchases. Works everywhere Visa/Mastercard is accepted.

**Payoneer** issues a Mastercard prepaid card. Slightly higher fees on foreign currency spending than the Wise card.

## Marketplace integrations

This is Payoneer's genuine advantage.

Payoneer has direct partnerships with: Upwork, Fiverr, Freelancer, Toptal, Amazon, Airbnb, Getty Images, Shutterstock, and 2,000+ other platforms and marketplaces. Receiving money from these platforms to Payoneer is typically free or charged at 1% (much lower than the 2-3% these platforms would otherwise charge to send international bank transfers).

Wise does not have these marketplace partnerships. Receiving from Upwork to Wise involves an international wire transfer with associated fees.

If a significant portion of your income comes from marketplaces, Payoneer's integration advantage is real money saved.

## Account limits and verification

**Wise** has straightforward KYC verification with clear limits that expand as you verify more. The process is quick and digital.

**Payoneer** has had some reports of account restrictions and verification delays, particularly for users in certain countries. Its onboarding is generally straightforward for users in its primary markets (US, EU, UK, Australia) but can be more complex for users in emerging markets.

## The fee summary

For a freelancer earning $50,000/year from direct client bank transfers, converting all income to GBP:

| Fee type | Wise | Payoneer |
|---|---|---|
| Receiving USD (ACH) | Free | 1% = $500 |
| Converting USD to GBP | ~0.5% = $250 | ~2% = $1,000 |
| Withdrawing to UK bank | Free | 2% = $1,000 |
| **Annual total** | **~$250** | **~$2,500** |

The difference is approximately $2,250 per year — money that stays in your pocket with Wise.

For the same freelancer earning primarily through Upwork, the receiving fees change significantly, and Payoneer's total cost becomes more competitive.

## The verdict

For most freelancers and remote workers receiving direct client payments, **Wise is substantially cheaper** — often by thousands per year at meaningful income levels. Its exchange rates are genuinely better, its fees are transparent, and the multi-currency account setup is as good as Payoneer's.

**Payoneer makes sense** if you rely on marketplace platforms that have free or low-cost Payoneer payouts, or if your clients are already on the Payoneer network. The platform integration advantage is real for this specific use case.

The most common optimal setup: Wise as your primary account for direct client payments and currency holding, Payoneer as a secondary account specifically to receive marketplace payments before converting via Wise.
