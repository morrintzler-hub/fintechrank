The UK has become the global capital of neobanking. Monzo, Revolut, Starling, and N26 between them serve over 30 million UK customers. All four are app-only, all four have no foreign transaction fees, and all four offer instant spending notifications. Beyond these basics, they are very different products built for very different users.

This is the comparison most guides get wrong — they compare feature lists without acknowledging that the right answer depends entirely on what you actually need from a bank account.

## Quick verdict

**Choose Monzo** for the best everyday UK current account — best budgeting tools, best customer support, best FSCS protection, and the most mature product for managing day-to-day finances.

**Choose Revolut** if you want a financial super-app covering banking, crypto, stock trading, currency exchange, and travel perks in one place, and you are willing to pay for a premium plan to access the best rates.

**Choose Starling** if you want a straightforward, no-frills current account with excellent UK banking fundamentals and a Marketplace of business integrations — particularly strong for freelancers and small businesses.

**Choose N26** if you are based in continental Europe and regularly move between countries — note that N26 exited the UK market in 2020 and is not available to UK residents.

## Regulatory standing and deposit protection

All three UK-available neobanks hold full UK banking licences, meaning deposits up to £85,000 are FSCS protected — the same protection as Barclays or HSBC.

**Monzo** received its full banking licence in 2017 and has approximately £10 billion in customer deposits.

**Starling** received its banking licence in 2016 and has been profitable since 2021, making it one of the few neobanks globally to achieve sustained profitability.

**Revolut** received its UK banking licence in July 2024 after a multi-year regulatory process — a significant milestone that puts UK deposits under FSCS protection for the first time. Before this, Revolut held funds as an e-money institution, which is safe but involves safeguarding rather than direct FSCS coverage.

## Fees and plans

All four offer a free tier. The differences in what you pay for on premium plans are meaningful.

| | Free plan | First paid plan | Premium plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monzo | Full current account | Plus £5/mo | Premium £15/mo |
| Revolut | Limited FX, basic features | Plus £2.99/mo | Premium £6.99/mo, Metal £12.99/mo |
| Starling | Full current account | No paid tier | No paid tier |
| N26 | Basic account | Smart EUR4.99/mo | You EUR9.99/mo |

Starling stands out for having no paid tiers for personal accounts — what you see is what you get. This simplicity is genuinely refreshing and means you never have to think about whether a feature is locked behind a paywall.

Monzo's paid plans add features like travel insurance, purchase protection, and higher ATM withdrawal limits abroad. The Plus plan at £5/month is good value if you travel regularly.

Revolut's free plan has a monthly currency exchange limit after which a 0.5-1% fee applies, and ATM withdrawals are limited to £200/month fee-free. The paid plans remove or raise these limits.

## Budgeting and money management

This is Monzo's strongest differentiator and the reason it remains the most-used neobank in the UK for primary banking.

**Monzo's** budgeting features are the most sophisticated of any UK bank, traditional or digital. Spending is automatically categorised (eating out, transport, shopping, entertainment, bills). You can set monthly spending limits per category and receive alerts as you approach them. The Salary Sorter automatically divides each pay cheque between bills, savings, and spending money. Savings Pots earn interest and can be locked until a target date. Round-ups add your spare change to a pot automatically.

**Starling** has solid spending categorisation and savings Spaces but lacks the sophistication and polish of Monzo's budgeting suite. It is better than most traditional banks but behind Monzo.

**Revolut** has spending analytics and budgeting tools that have improved significantly, but they remain secondary features in an app designed primarily around currency exchange and financial products.

## Customer support

Monzo's customer support is consistently rated the best of any bank — traditional or digital — in the UK. It is available 24/7 via in-app chat with response times that are genuinely fast. Complex issues are handled knowledgeably and with empathy. This consistency over years is the result of deliberate investment and a customer-centric culture.

Starling's support is also strong, particularly for business customers where the team has deep expertise.

Revolut's support on the free plan has historically been its weakest point — response times on complex issues can run to days rather than hours. On paid plans, priority support is available and meaningfully better.

## Travel and international use

All three are excellent for travel. No foreign transaction fees and good exchange rates on everyday spending.

**Revolut** is the strongest for frequent travellers on paid plans — airport lounge access, travel insurance, higher ATM limits abroad, and access to 36 currencies at interbank rates. For someone who travels monthly for work, the Metal plan at £12.99/month can pay for itself in a single trip.

**Monzo** is solid for travel with no foreign transaction fees and fee-free ATM withdrawals up to £200/month (free plan) or more on paid plans. The Plus plan includes travel insurance.

**Starling** offers no foreign transaction fees and fee-free ATM withdrawals anywhere in the world — genuinely unlimited, which is Starling's travel differentiator on the free plan. No travel insurance on the free tier.

## Business banking

**Starling** is the standout choice for business banking. Its business current account is free, integrates with accounting software (Xero, FreeAgent, QuickBooks), and has been voted Best British Business Bank multiple years running. The Marketplace allows businesses to connect payroll, insurance, and financial services tools.

**Monzo** offers a business account at £5/month for sole traders and £9/month for limited companies. Features are solid but less comprehensive than Starling for business use.

**Revolut Business** is a separate, more powerful product designed for high-growth companies — multi-currency accounts, mass payments, and spend management tools. It is not really the same category as Monzo or Starling business banking.

## Which should you choose?

The right answer depends on what you actually use a bank account for.

**If you want one bank account for everything:** Monzo. It is the most complete everyday banking product, the budgeting tools are unmatched, and the customer support is the best in the industry. Put your salary here and use Monzo's Pots for savings goals.

**If you travel frequently or want crypto and investing alongside banking:** Revolut Premium or Metal. The combined value of travel insurance, lounge access, and higher currency exchange limits pays for the subscription for most frequent travellers.

**If you run a freelance or small business:** Starling. The free business account, accounting integrations, and Marketplace are the best in the UK for this use case.

**If you already use Monzo or Starling:** Consider adding Wise as a second account for international transfers specifically. The combination covers everything.

What none of these accounts gives you that traditional banks still provide: physical branches, cash handling, and relationship managers for complex financial needs. If you regularly deposit cash or need in-person banking, you will still need a high street bank account alongside your neobank.
