Revolut has over 50 million customers worldwide, processes billions in transactions every month, and is now one of the most valuable private fintech companies in Europe. And yet the question keeps coming up: *is Revolut actually safe?*

It is a fair question. Revolut spent several years operating as an e-money institution rather than a licensed bank, which meant your deposits were not protected the same way as a traditional bank account. That changed in 2024. But there are still things worth understanding before you move your savings there.

Here is the honest breakdown.

## The short answer

Yes - for most people, Revolut is safe for everyday use. For large savings, there are still reasons to be cautious, but they are manageable.

## Revolut's regulatory status in 2026

**UK:** Revolut received its full UK banking licence from the PRA in July 2024, after a three-year application process. This means UK customer deposits up to **£85,000 are now FSCS protected** - the same protection you get at Barclays or HSBC. This was the biggest outstanding concern about Revolut's safety, and it has been resolved.

**EU:** Revolut operates under a Lithuanian banking licence (granted in 2021) across the EU. EU deposits are protected up to **€100,000** under the European Deposit Guarantee Scheme.

**US:** Revolut operates as a money services business in the US. Deposits are **not** FDIC insured in the same way as a US bank. US customers should be aware of this distinction.

**Rest of world:** Revolut holds various e-money and payment licences depending on the market. Protection varies by jurisdiction - check your local terms.

## How does the FSCS protection actually work?

If Revolut UK were to fail, the FSCS would compensate eligible customers up to £85,000 per person. This is the same guarantee that covers your Nationwide or Lloyds account. It is backed by the UK government and has never failed to pay out a valid claim.

The key caveat: the £85,000 limit applies *per banking institution*, not per account. If you already have £85,000 at another FSCS-protected bank, your total protection across both is still £85,000 at each - they are separate limits.

## What about before the banking licence?

Before July 2024, UK Revolut operated as an e-money institution. Under that structure, your money was held in segregated accounts at established banks rather than used for lending. It was protected from Revolut's creditors if the company went bust, but it was **not** FSCS insured.

During that period, using Revolut for large amounts carried more risk than a licensed bank. That risk is now largely resolved for UK customers.

## Revolut's security features

On the technical security side, Revolut is strong:

**Card controls:** You can freeze and unfreeze your card instantly, disable online payments, contactless or ATM withdrawals individually, and set spending limits - all from the app in seconds. These controls are faster and more granular than most traditional banks.

**Virtual cards:** Revolut lets you create disposable virtual card numbers for online shopping. The number works once and then expires, meaning your real card details are never exposed to merchants.

**Transaction notifications:** Every transaction triggers an instant push notification. If a payment you did not make appears, you see it immediately.

**Biometric authentication:** The app supports Face ID and fingerprint login. You can also enable a PIN for in-app transfers.

**Location-based security:** You can restrict card use to your current location, which makes remote card fraud harder.

## What are the remaining concerns?

**Customer support:** Revolut's customer support is entirely in-app and can be slow to reach a human. For traditional banking problems - a disputed transaction, a frozen account - this can be frustrating. Monzo consistently outperforms Revolut on customer support responsiveness. If you need a bank where you can call someone, Revolut is not it.

**Account freezes:** There have been well-documented cases of Revolut freezing accounts, often as a result of automated fraud detection flagging unusual activity. Revolut's fraud systems are aggressive, and legitimate transactions can trigger a freeze. This is an industry-wide issue - Monzo and Starling have similar problems - but Revolut has faced more complaints about difficulty resolving frozen accounts quickly.

**Business account limitations:** For business accounts specifically, Revolut's terms of service include broader rights to suspend or close accounts than most traditional banks. If you run a business, read the terms carefully before making Revolut your primary business account.

**Not suitable for large savings:** Even with FSCS protection up to £85,000, keeping very large amounts at any single institution carries concentration risk. If you have significant savings, spreading them across two or three institutions is sound practice regardless of which banks you use.

## The bottom line

Revolut is safe for everyday spending and routine banking in 2026. The UK banking licence resolved the most significant protection gap, and Revolut's security features are genuinely excellent.

The remaining caveats - slow customer support, aggressive account freezes, and the US deposit situation - are real but manageable if you go in aware of them. Keep your day-to-day spending money there, enjoy the best exchange rates and card controls available, and maintain a separate account at a traditional bank for your main savings if the amounts are significant.
