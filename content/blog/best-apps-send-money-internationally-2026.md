The World Bank has tracked the cost of sending money across borders for more than a decade, and the global average has barely budged. It still sits above **6%** of the amount sent. What makes that number remarkable is not its size but its invisibility: most people who pay it never see a line item for it on any receipt.

*So if the fee is not on the receipt, where is it?*

It is in the exchange rate. When a bank quotes you a rate to convert dollars into euros, that rate is not the one banks use among themselves. It is that rate plus a margin, and the margin is revenue. A transfer advertised as free can quietly cost **4%** of the principal, while a transfer charging a visible **$4.50** fee can be cheaper on the same amount. The headline fee tells you almost nothing.

Here is the honest breakdown.

## The short answer

For most people moving money between major currencies, [Wise](/review/wise) is the correct default: the real **mid-market rate** and a visible fee, usually **0.4%** to **1%**. If the recipient needs cash, or needs it within minutes, [Remitly](/review/remitly) and [WorldRemit](/review/worldremit) are usually better. If you are a freelancer invoicing clients abroad rather than supporting family, [Payoneer](/review/payoneer) solves a different problem well. If you already bank with [Revolut](/review/revolut), it is fine for occasional transfers inside your allowance. [PayPal](/review/paypal) works everywhere and costs more than it should.

## What the mid-market rate means, and how to check it

The mid-market rate is the midpoint between the buy and sell prices for a currency pair on the global market. It is the rate you see on Google, on Reuters, and on XE. No retail customer is strictly entitled to it, but it is the honest reference point, and the gap between it and your quoted rate is the real cost of the transfer.

Checking takes thirty seconds and is the single most useful habit in this area. Search the pair, note the rate, then read the rate your provider quotes for your amount. Divide the difference by the mid-market rate, add any visible fee, and that total is what you pay. It is the only figure worth comparing.

Bank markups are largest on small amounts, and they widen at weekends, when the underlying market is closed.

## Wise - best overall

Wise publishes the mid-market rate and charges its fee separately and visibly, which is the whole argument for it. The fee typically lands between **0.4%** and **1%**, lower on high-volume corridors such as USD to EUR and higher on thinner ones. It covers more than **160** countries and gives you local account details in several currencies, so you can receive as well as send.

The drawbacks are real. Not every transfer is instant: some corridors settle in seconds, others take a full business day, and large amounts can trigger verification. Funding by card costs more than funding by bank transfer, which is easy to miss at checkout. We compared it directly in [Wise vs Payoneer](/blog/wise-vs-payoneer-2026).

## Remitly - best for cash pickup and speed

Remitly is built for remittance corridors rather than general currency conversion, and it shows. Its network on US to Mexico and UK to Philippines is strong, cash pickup is widely available, and delivery can be within minutes.

Understand the two-tier pricing. Economy is cheaper and slower; Express costs more and is faster. Choosing Express by reflex when nobody needs the money today is how people overpay here.

## Payoneer - best for freelancers and businesses

Payoneer is not really a remittance app. It gives you local receiving accounts in **USD**, **EUR** and **GBP**, so a client in New York can pay you by domestic transfer and you receive it as if you held a local account. For contractors billing international clients, that removes real friction.

The cost sits at the edges: withdrawal fees apply when you move money to your own bank, and in-platform conversion is not free. For sending money to a relative, it is the wrong tool.

## WorldRemit - good for Africa and Asia corridors

WorldRemit has a large agent network and supports mobile money wallets, which matters enormously where mobile money is the main account most people hold. Into much of Africa and parts of Asia it is often the most practical option rather than merely the cheapest. Rates vary by corridor more than Wise's do, so run the check every time.

## Revolut - convenient for existing users

If your money already sits in [Revolut](/review/revolut), transfers are fast and the rate is fair up to your plan's monthly conversion allowance. Beyond it a markup applies, and a further markup applies at weekends. Neither is hidden, but both are easy to forget. See [Revolut vs Wise vs Monzo](/blog/revolut-vs-wise-vs-monzo-2026) and, on the question we are asked most, [is Revolut safe](/blog/is-revolut-safe-2026).

## PayPal - widely accepted but expensive

PayPal's advantage is reach: almost everyone has an account, so nobody needs new onboarding. Its rates are poor, frequently several percent above mid-market, and the conversion step is where the cost lands rather than the stated fee. Reasonable for one small urgent transfer. Poor for anything regular.

## How to choose

The corridor matters more than the brand. A provider that is excellent from the United States to Germany can be mediocre from Canada to Nigeria, because pricing depends on local partnerships rather than general reputation. Check your own pair and your own amount rather than trusting a ranking, including this one.

Then decide what you are optimising for. If the money is not needed today, the slower and cheaper option is almost always right, and the difference compounds for anyone sending regularly. If someone is waiting, paying for speed is a legitimate purchase. Confirm how the recipient wants to be paid before you pick an app, because the best rate available is worthless if the money lands somewhere they cannot reach.

## The bottom line

The 6% average survives because most people never check, and the providers relying on that will not volunteer the number. Checking it is the whole skill. Use Wise for most transfers between major currencies, Remitly or WorldRemit when the recipient needs cash or sits on a corridor those networks serve better, and keep Payoneer for invoicing. Compare the quoted rate against mid-market before you confirm, every time, and the hidden fee stops being hidden - which is the only reliable way to stop paying it.
